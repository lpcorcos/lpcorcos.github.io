Broadly, my research interests include mathematical modeling and scientific computing. During my Ph.D., I specialized in numerical partial differential equations (PDEs) and developed tailored high-order accurate algorithms for multi-physics interface problems. I also study numerical linear algebra and recently released BlockSp – A templated C++ library for block sparse linear algebra.

Here, three distinct fluids layered on top of each other undergo an effect known as Interfacial Turbulence, i.e., the spontaneous agitation of an interface between two unequilibrated liquids. Here the matter transfers upward from the bottom layer, is captured by the next interfaces, and is driven in an hydrodynamically unstable manner by the Marangoni effect.

Multi-Layer Coating Flows

The above is an example of a multi-layer coating, a complex multi-physics problem driven by multi-phase Marangoni interfacial fluid flow and the transport, mixing, and evaporation of multiple dissolved species. We developed a mathematical model and numerical framework for this challenging problem. The hybrid algorithm combines finite difference level set methods and high-order accurate sharp interface implicit mesh discontinuous Galerkin methods; the algorithm is high-order accurate in space and first order in time.

The framework is applicable to an arbitrary number of layers and dissolved species. In our paper, we apply it in a variety of settings, including multi-solvent evaporative paint dynamics, the flow and leveling of multi-layer automobile paint coatings in both 2D and 3D, and interfacial turbulence within a multi-layer matter cascade. Our results reproduce several phenomena observed in experiment, such as the formation of Marangoni plumes and Bénard cells. These can be seen in the images below. The work was informed by experiments from PPG Industries Inc. as part of our collaboration under the Department of Energy's HPC4Mfg project.

Discontinuous Galerkin Methods

As part of my work on multi-physics multi-layer coating flows, I developed local discontinuous Galerkin methods (LDG) for Poisson problems with Robin boundary conditions. For this, consider a \(d\)-dimensional domain \( \Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d \), with Robin boundary conditions applied along the boundary \( \Gamma_R \), we wish to find \(u:\Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \) such that

\[ \begin{cases} -\nabla \cdot ( \mu \nabla u) = f &\text{ in } \Omega \\ au + (b \mu \nabla u)\cdot \textbf{n} = r &\text{ on } \Gamma_R, \end{cases} \] where \(\mu\) is the diffusion coefficient, \(a,b\) are Robin coefficients, \(f,r\) are source terms, and \(\textbf{n} \) is the normal vector to \(\Gamma_R \).

Our resulting LDG discretization has several favorable properties: it is optimally high-order accurate in the maximum norm and the final linear system is symmetric positive definite. Moreover, the LDG discretization is amenable to straightforward and fast multigrid solvers. These LDG methods are used with the Implicit Mesh DG framework to apply Robin boundary conditions along curved interfaces defined implicitly via the level set method, which are in turn used to capture the effects of evaporation within multi-layer coating flow problems, to high-order accuracy. The figure illustrates the LDG method's convergence rates and multigrid performance in a challenging case of variable diffusion and Robin coefficients spanning several orders of magnitude on a curved implicitly-defined domain.

Additionally, I developed high-order accurate LDG methods for Poisson problems with Robin jump conditions as well as for Stokes problems with Navier-slip boundary conditions. These can be found in my thesis.

Reference L. P. Corcos, A Hybrid Finite Difference Level Set–Implicit Mesh Discontinuous Galerkin Method for Multi-Layer Coating Flow Problems, December 2023, University of California Berkeley. Link  PDF

Cavity Quantum Electrodynamics (QED)

As an undergraduate researcher at the University of Maryland, College Park, I worked in the Cavity Quantum Electrodynamics (QED) laboratory of Professor Luis Orozco and was a member of the Joint Quantum Institute (JQI). In that work, we measured the quantum speed-up of the state evolution in an optical cavity QED system and demonstrated non-Markovian behavior.

Reference AD Cimmarusti, Z Yan, BD Patterson, LP Corcos, LA Orozco, S Deffner, Environment-assisted speed-up of the field evolution in cavity quantum electrodynamics, Physical review letters 114 (23), 233602 (2015). Link   PDF
